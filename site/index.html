Little stamps. Big ideas.

A Mario Paint-inspired music composer. Pick a character stamp, press a key, and a note lands on the staff. Each of the 31 stamps is its own sound, so a song is a picture you can hear.

01 / The idea

A little composer for a little synth

Stamp Composer turns the FM-1 into a music maker in the spirit of Mario Paint. The keys place notes, the knobs shape them, and eight layers let you stack parts. It is built on the open fm1-base platform, and the same C code that runs on the unit also runs in your browser.

  • 31sounds, one per stamp
  • 8layers to arrange with
  • 384notes per song
  • 8song slots, plus autosave
  • 16steps of undo and redo
  • MIDIchannel for every sound

Also on board: a staff with flats and sharps, triplet grids, swing and humanize, bar copy, move and transpose, MIDI clock in and out, and a Twinkle Twinkle demo to start from.

05 / Your first song

Your first song in 2 minutes

  1. Turn PRESETS to pick a character. Press a few keys: each one plays and places a note at the cursor. Press a note again to remove it.
  2. Turn SELECT to move the cursor one step along, and place another note. Press several keys together for a chord.
  3. Press SEQ to change the step size, then add quicker notes.
  4. Press PLAY to hear it. Press it again to stop. Not happy? ENV undoes, LFO redoes.
  5. Turn ALGORITHM to pick another layer, pick another stamp with PRESETS, and add a second part.
  6. Turn KNOB1 for the tempo and press ARP to loop. Press SAVE to keep it in a slot. It is also saved on its own after a few quiet seconds.

Want a clean page? Press GLO, turn KNOB1 to a slot that says EMPTY and press REC twice. No FM-1 at hand? Try it in your browser, or open the quick preview, which also imports and exports songs as .scf files.

06 / Install

Ready for your FM-1

Install

  1. Keep M-VAVE's stock firmware file somewhere safe, so you can always go back.
  2. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer, a data USB cable, and leave the FM-1 powered from USB.
  3. Open the installer, allow MIDI access, and pick your FM-1.
  4. Leave "also write m-bootloader" unticked. That replaces the boot loader, and this project has not tested it.
  5. Press install and do not unplug until the page says it is done.

Open the installer

Back up, and go back

  • Backup saves all eight song slots to one file and restores them all together.
  • The installer's Back to the stock firmware writes M-VAVE's firmware over the same cable.
  • If a unit will not start, it falls into safe mode, and a rescue tool can write stock firmware back. The manual's recovery section and the install notes explain each step.

Not tested on a physical FM-1 yet. Read the install notes before you flash.

07 / Developed in the open

What has been tested, and what has not

Tested

  • The core engine and importer, with unit tests (tests/run_tests.sh).
  • The whole app on a host simulator driven by scripts: every page, saving, autosave, MIDI, undo, bars, groove.
  • The real firmware package, booted and played in the fm1-emu emulator in your browser.
  • The same C source compiled to WebAssembly for the quick preview.

Not tested

  • Anything on a physical FM-1: real boot, flash, audio output, input feel, MIDI with real gear.

08 / Questions

Good to know

Where do the sounds and the pictures come from?

The stamps and the sound pack come from Nintendo's Mario Paint, by way of the Super Mario Paint project. They are not covered by this project's MIT licence, and this is an unaffiliated fan project. They live in an asset pack you can swap: build with STAMP_BANK=placeholder for original tones, or point STAMP_WAV_DIR at your own WAV files.

Will it break my FM-1? How do I go back?

Installing replaces M-VAVE's firmware, so keep the stock file first. The installer can write it back over the same cable, M-VAVE's own updater works too, and if a start fails twice the unit offers a safe mode. The firmware has not been tested on a physical FM-1 yet, so please read the recovery notes before you begin.

Does it do MIDI?

Yes. Every sound can send on its own channel (or all of them can), with velocity, length and tune. There is MIDI clock out, and clock in that makes the FM-1 follow another machine. Notes coming in over USB or the MIDI jack act like keys. The MIDI page has all of it.

Can I bring songs from Super Mario Paint?

Yes, in two steps. The project's importer (tools/import_smp.py) turns a Super Mario Paint song into a .scf file, and the quick preview opens it with Import a .scf song, where you can listen, edit and export it.

Do the keys have to play the stamp I picked?

Yes: the stamp is the sound. Different stamps on different layers make a band. In FREE PLAY (OCT− and OCT+ together) the keys only play and never write, which is handy for jamming over a song.
